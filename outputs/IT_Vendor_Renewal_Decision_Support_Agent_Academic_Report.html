Abstract

Executive Summary: Modern enterprise IT organisations face mounting software expenditure, contractual compliance complexities, and operational inefficiencies during annual software vendor renewals. Manual contract analysis is labour-intensive and frequently suffers from four systemic vulnerabilities: unmonitored licence shelfware, unrecovered service level agreement (SLA) penalty credits, missed notice deadlines triggering inadvertent auto-renewals, and understated migration costs during vendor replacement appraisals.

To address these industrial challenges, this project presents the IT Vendor Renewal Decision Support Agent, an automated, deterministic, evidence-based decision support system built to assist IT, Procurement, Legal, and Finance leadership in conducting rigorous, multi-dimensional renewal evaluations. The system accepts contract documents across multiple formats (PDF, DOCX, and TXT), licence usage logs (CSV, Excel), historical SLA incident records, and structured vendor quotations. It coordinates specialized analytical engines to extract contractual terms via pattern recognition, evaluate seat-level licence utilisation against configurable thresholds, compute SLA downtime credits using fixed financial rules, monitor renewal notice deadlines with multi-stage urgency warnings, and perform multi-option total-cost-of-ownership (TCO) comparisons across three standard renewal pathways: Renew, Renegotiate, and Replace.

Crucially, the system enforces a strict advisory-only boundary. All financial arithmetic is computed using exact decimal arithmetic (via Python’s decimal.Decimal) to prevent IEEE-754 floating-point inaccuracies. Cross-vendor information leakage is strictly prevented by isolating document clause indexing via vendor-specific BM25 search spaces. Built entirely on local deterministic algorithms without dependence on external large language model (LLM) APIs, the system completely eliminates generative hallucination risks while guaranteeing total corporate confidentiality. The system was verified against a comprehensive automated test suite consisting of 101 automated test cases achieving an 84% code coverage rate, including formal specification tests covering unpriced migration blocks, severe SLA penalty calculations, missing contract error handling, and deterministic rejection of unauthorized contractual action commands. The final assessment artifact is immutably hashed using SHA-256 and presented via both an interactive Streamlit web dashboard and standalone executive audit reports.

Keywords: Decision Support Systems (DSS), IT Procurement, Software Contract Renewal, Service Level Agreements (SLA), Licence Utilisation, Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), Deterministic Financial Computing, Safe Advisory Governance.

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background and Domain Context

Modern enterprise architectures rely heavily on commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) and Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platforms to power mission-critical business workflows. From enterprise resource planning (ERP) and customer relationship management (CRM) systems to cloud infrastructure, communication suites, and cybersecurity tooling, organizations frequently maintain dozens, if not hundreds, of distinct commercial software contracts simultaneously.

Each software relationship is governed by a legally binding master services agreement (MSA), service level agreement (SLA), and commercial quotation schedule. These contracts establish specific operational parameters, including committed uptimes, financial service credit remedies for downtime, mandatory renewal notice notification windows, auto-renewal triggers, and tiered pricing structures. Despite the immense capital allocated to these agreements—often representing 20% to 35% of an organization's annual operational IT budget—the administrative management of renewals remains overwhelmingly manual, decentralized, and reactive across modern corporate enterprises.

1.2 Project Motivation

In standard enterprise procurement lifecycles, vendor contracts are negotiated, signed, and then filed in disparate repositories or shared file drives. As the expiration date approaches, procurement officers and IT managers are tasked with answering several high-stakes questions:

  • Are our employees actively utilizing the licences we procured, or are we paying annual maintenance on dormant "shelfware"?
  • Did the vendor satisfy their contractual availability commitments, and are we legally entitled to claw back uncollected SLA penalty credits?
  • What is the hard legal deadline by which we must submit formal written notice of non-renewal to avoid a punitive automatic one-year extension?
  • If an alternative vendor offers an apparently cheaper annual subscription, does the hidden overhead of data migration, staff retraining, and integration make replacement uneconomical?

Answering these questions manually requires cross-referencing multi-page legal documents against telemetry usage spreadsheets, incident ticket logs, and vendor proposal quotes. Due to severe time constraints, procurement teams frequently default to renewing agreements "as-is," resulting in substantial wasted capital, continuous SLA breaches without accountability, and lost negotiating leverage.

1.3 Problem Statement

Manual enterprise IT software vendor renewal reviews are time-consuming, fragmented, and vulnerable to human error. Organizations lack an integrated, deterministic analytical tool that can concurrently ingest unstructured legal contracts, parse telemetry logs, compute exact SLA financial credits, flag looming notice deadlines, and rigorously evaluate total-cost-of-ownership (TCO) comparisons. Furthermore, existing commercial contract intelligence tools often rely on opaque, non-deterministic cloud AI APIs that risk exposing confidential pricing terms to external servers and introduce generative hallucinations into legal interpretations.

1.4 Project Objectives

The core objective of this project is to research, design, develop, and thoroughly verify an offline, deterministic decision support system—the IT Vendor Renewal Decision Support Agent. Specific sub-objectives include:

  1. Multi-Format Document Parsing: Ingest vendor legal contracts across PDF, Word (DOCX), and Plain Text (TXT) formats without relying on external cloud conversion services.
  2. Pattern-Based Term Extraction: Extract key contract metadata (effective date, renewal date, notice period, SLA target, credit percentage, seat count) and assign confidence indicators without black-box language models.
  3. Isolated Contract Search: Provide keyword and BM25 clause retrieval strictly bounded to the selected vendor to eliminate cross-vendor confidential data leakage.
  4. Seat-Level Utilisation Analytics: Compute active vs. allocated seat percentages from CSV/Excel usage logs and flag underutilised subscriptions relative to configurable thresholds.
  5. Deterministic SLA Credit Computation: Correlate contractual SLA targets against incident logs to calculate valid downtime penalties and monetary service credit clawbacks using exact decimal arithmetic.
  6. Deadline Monitoring & Alerting: Calculate critical notice deadlines and classify renewal urgency into tiered operational alerts (CRITICAL, WARNING, OK).
  7. Comparative TCO Financial Analysis: Model three standardized decision options (Renew, Renegotiate, Replace), with mandatory safeguards against unpriced replacement migration costs.
  8. Advisory-Only Governance: Implement deterministic safety filters that reject any user command requesting automated contract execution, cancellation, or termination.

1.5 Scope of the Project

The system is scoped as a corporate decision support system (DSS) designed for desktop or on-premises intranet deployment. It supports procurement managers, IT directors, legal counsel, and financial analysts by delivering structured analytical dashboards, traceable evidence references, and exportable executive markdown/HTML reports.

1.6 Limitations and Operational Boundaries

The project operates under explicit architectural boundaries:

  • Advisory Role: The software acts purely as an analytical assistant. It provides recommendations with supporting evidence, but cannot and will not sign, renew, modify, or terminate contracts.
  • Scanned Document OCR: The system relies on native text layer extraction (via PDFPlumber). Scanned, image-only contracts generate explicit warning flags indicating that manual OCR or digital text conversion is required.
  • Static Rule Extraction: Contract terms are extracted using curated regular expression patterns. Highly non-standard legal wording that deviates significantly from standard terminology will receive a "LOW" or "NOT_FOUND" confidence score, deliberately prompting human legal review rather than guessing.

Chapter 2: Existing Process and Proposed Solution

2.1 Current Industrial Renewal Workflows

In the absence of dedicated automation, corporate software renewals typically follow a frantic, multi-week manual cycle:

  1. An enterprise renewal alert is triggered—often only 15 to 30 days prior to contract expiration—when an account executive sends an updated invoice or quotation.
  2. A procurement officer attempts to locate the original master contract across local folder shares, email archives, or physical filing cabinets.
  3. An IT systems administrator is contacted via email to manually pull active user counts from identity providers (e.g., Active Directory, Okta, or CSV exports).
  4. If service quality was substandard, service desk logs must be manually filtered to calculate total annual downtime hours and check whether SLA credit claims are still contractually permissible.
  5. Alternative vendor pricing is solicited, but procurement teams frequently compare only the headline subscription costs, omitting the substantial professional services, data export, and parallel operating costs required for migration.

2.2 Common Enterprise Renewal Pitfalls

This manual approach exposes organizations to four systemic financial and legal risks:

Pitfall Category Operational Reality & Impact Direct Financial Consequence
Unmonitored Shelfware (Low Utilisation) Organizations purchase software bundles for all employees, but departmental churn or role changes result in licences sitting idle. Procurement renews the full seat count due to lack of visibility. Paying 100% maintenance on 60%–70% active usage; wasting tens of thousands of dollars annually on inactive seats.
Unrecovered SLA Penalty Credits Vendors commit to 99.9% availability with contractual remedies (e.g., 5%–10% invoice credits per breach). Because claiming credits requires manual documentation within 30 days, credits are rarely claimed. Forfeiture of legitimate financial compensation; subsidising poor vendor infrastructure reliability.
Missed Notice Windows & Auto-Renewal Enterprise contracts frequently mandate 30, 60, or 90 days advance written notice to terminate. Procurement teams unaware of the notice date miss the deadline by days. Automatic legally binding one-year contract extension at standard or increased rack rates without negotiating leverage.
Unaccounted Migration Overheads Alternative vendors present attractive annual licence quotes. Procurement selects "Replace" without factoring in data migration, API rebuilds, and staff retraining expenses. Severe budget overruns; first-year total cost of replacement significantly exceeds the cost of staying with the incumbent.

2.3 Proposed Decision Support Solution

The IT Vendor Renewal Decision Support Agent replaces this disorganized, error-prone manual procedure with a consolidated, deterministic computational pipeline. By ingesting all four core data sources—contract documents, telemetry logs, SLA incident histories, and vendor quotations—the system performs concurrent automated analyses:

  • Extracts and displays contract terms alongside extraction confidence scores and exact text evidence snippets.
  • Calculates exact seat-level utilisation percentages, instantly identifying surplus licences eligible for de-scoping during renegotiation.
  • Parses incident records to identify qualifying SLA outages, calculates downtime percentages, and tallies exact monetary service credit clawbacks.
  • Calculates the precise notice cutoff date and displays color-coded deadline countdown alerts.
  • Computes total annual and switching costs across Renew, Renegotiate, and Replace options, enforcing a mandatory warning block if replacement migration costs are unpriced.
  • Synthesizes all findings into a deterministic recommendation backed by transparent evidence and audit citations.

Chapter 3: Requirements Analysis

3.1 Functional Requirements (FR)

The system's operational capabilities are defined by nine primary functional requirements:

  • FR-01: Multi-Format Contract Ingestion: The system must accept contract files in PDF (.pdf), Microsoft Word (.docx), and Plain Text (.txt) formats. It must validate file existence and integrity, returning informative errors if a file is missing or corrupted.
  • FR-02: Pattern-Based Term Extraction: The system must extract core contract fields (Vendor Name, Effective Date, Expiry/Renewal Date, Notice Period Days, Annual Value, Total Licences, SLA Availability Target, and SLA Credit Percentage) using curated regular expression heuristics, assigning confidence levels (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, NOT_FOUND) and text evidence snippets.
  • FR-03: Isolated Clause Retrieval: The system must index parsed contract sections into a local Vector Space / BM25 search engine, strictly segregating indexed clauses by vendor name to prevent cross-vendor search contamination.
  • FR-04: Seat Utilisation Evaluation: The system must parse employee usage records, compute active vs. allocated seat percentages, and classify utilisation against a configurable threshold (default: 80%). It must quantify surplus licences and potential de-scoping cost savings.
  • FR-05: SLA Breach & Credit Calculation: The system must ingest incident logs, compute monthly/annual downtime, identify contractual availability breaches, and calculate exact monetary service credits owed according to contract credit clauses.
  • FR-06: Renewal Notice Deadline Monitoring: The system must compute the exact legal notice deadline by subtracting the notice period days from the expiration date, categorizing status into OVERDUE, CRITICAL (≤ 14 days), WARNING (≤ 30 days), or OK.
  • FR-07: Three-Way Cost Modeling: The system must evaluate and compare three pathways—Renew (status quo), Renegotiate (optimised seats/credits), and Replace (alternative vendor). If an alternative vendor quotation lacks migration/implementation costs, it must halt definitive recommendation and issue an unpriced migration warning.
  • FR-08: Advisory Safeguards & Forbidden Action Interception: The system must intercept and reject user prompts or commands demanding autonomous contract actions (e.g., "terminate now", "auto-renew agreement", "execute cancellation"), returning a strict advisory boundary disclaimer.
  • FR-09: Audit Storage & Multi-Format Reporting: The system must serialize complete assessment states into timestamped JSON files with SHA-256 integrity hashes, and generate executive reports in both Markdown and HTML formats.

3.2 Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)

  • NFR-01: Computational Determinism: Identical input data must consistently yield identical financial totals, credit calculations, and recommendations across all platforms without stochastic variance.
  • NFR-02: Zero Cloud Reliance (Privacy & Sovereignty): All data processing, contract parsing, search indexing, and report generation must occur entirely on the local host. No telemetry, contracts, or credentials may be transmitted across external networks.
  • NFR-03: Sub-Second Analytical Latency: Full multi-dimensional analysis across contracts, usage records, incidents, and quotations must complete in under 500 milliseconds on standard desktop hardware.
  • NFR-04: Robust Error Handling: Missing files, corrupt spreadsheets, or unreadable contract clauses must never crash the application. The system must degrade gracefully, issuing structured warning alerts while continuing remaining analyses.
  • NFR-05: Responsive and Accessible Presentation: The graphical user interface must provide clear visual hierarchies, intuitive tab navigation, accessible contrast ratios, and seamless responsiveness across desktop viewports.

4.2 Modular Decomposition and Component Responsibilities

4.2.1 Contract Reader & Extraction Module (contract_reader.py, contract_extractor.py)

Responsible for safely loading physical contract files and extracting critical metadata. contract_reader.py handles low-level file I/O for TXT, PDF (using pdfplumber), and DOCX (using python-docx). It verifies file existence, catches parser exceptions, and detects scanned image PDFs lacking a text stream, returning structured warnings. contract_extractor.py processes the extracted text using compiled regex patterns for dates, currency amounts, seat counts, notice periods, and percentages, calculating a confidence rating and supporting text excerpt for each field.

4.2.2 Isolated Clause Search Engine (retriever.py)

Implements an in-memory lexical BM25 / TF-IDF information retrieval engine. The contract is partitioned into indexed sections or paragraphs. Crucially, each index is strictly tagged and filtered by vendor_name. When searching for sensitive terms (e.g., "service credits", "termination liability"), queries only score chunks belonging to the selected vendor, mathematically preventing cross-vendor data leakage.

4.2.3 Licence Utilisation Analyser (utilisation.py)

Parses telemetry spreadsheets to quantify licence consumption. It calculates: 

Utilisation Rate (%) = (Active Licences / Total Procured Licences) * 100
Surplus Licences = Total Procured Licences - Active Licences
Potential Savings = Surplus Licences * Unit Price

If the utilisation rate falls below the configurable threshold (default: 80%), it flags the account for licence rightsizing and renegotiation.

4.2.4 SLA Breach & Credit Analyser (sla_analyzer.py)

Correlates recorded incident downtime against contractual availability commitments. It computes: 

Actual Availability (%) = ((Total Period Minutes - Total Downtime Minutes) / Total Period Minutes) * 100
SLA Margin = Contractual Target (%) - Actual Availability (%)
Service Credit ($) = Annual Contract Value * (Credit Percentage / 100)

It generates an itemized table of breach incidents, providing procurement with indisputable evidence for credit clawbacks during renewal negotiations.

4.2.5 Renewal Deadline Monitor (deadline_monitor.py)

Calculates the legally enforceable notice cutoff date: 

Notice Cutoff Date = Contract Expiry Date - Notice Period (Days)
Days Remaining = Notice Cutoff Date - Current Assessment Date

It evaluates urgency into discrete operational states:

  • OVERDUE: Cutoff date has already passed. High risk of auto-renewal.
  • CRITICAL: ≤ 14 days remaining. Immediate executive intervention required.
  • WARNING: 15 to 30 days remaining. Active negotiation window.
  • OK: > 30 days remaining. Adequate preparation time.

4.2.6 Multi-Option Cost Comparator (cost_comparator.py)

Evaluates the three commercial options. For the Replace option, it enforces a strict rule: if an alternative vendor's quotation omits migration or implementation costs, it marks the option as incomplete, sets recommendation_pending = True, and issues an explicit warning banner. This prevents executive leadership from approving a replacement that appears cheaper on paper but conceals massive migration deficits.

4.2.7 Safety Guardrails & Orchestrator (agent.py)

Serves as the central coordinator. It executes the entire analytical pipeline, compiles individual component results into a unified AssessmentResult data container, and enforces advisory safety boundaries. The function check_forbidden_action() intercepts imperative commands seeking autonomous contract changes, ensuring the tool remains strictly advisory.

4.2.8 Assessment Store & Report Generator (assessment_store.py, report_generator.py)

Saves the completed assessment into an immutable JSON file named with the vendor ID, timestamp, and a SHA-256 integrity hash. report_generator.py converts the structured assessment data into standardized executive markdown and styled HTML documents.

Chapter 6: Methodology and Implementation

6.1 End-to-End Execution Flow

The system executes a structured five-stage data processing pipeline:

  1. Stage 1: Document & Data Ingestion
    The user selects an existing vendor profile or uploads raw files via the Streamlit interface (or passes paths to the CLI). The system validates file extensions and triggers the appropriate parser: pdfplumber for PDFs, python-docx for DOCX, or native UTF-8 file streams for TXT.
  2. Stage 2: Pattern-Based Extraction & Chunk Indexing
    The contract text is normalized and scanned against pre-compiled regular expressions. Identified terms are assigned confidence ratings based on surrounding contextual keywords (e.g., matching "30 days" preceded by "written notice" yields HIGH confidence). Concurrently, text blocks are indexed into the local BM25 retriever tagged with the active vendor ID.
  3. Stage 3: Telemetry, SLA, and Deadline Processing
    The system reads the associated licence usage logs, computing active login percentages and surplus counts. Next, incident logs are parsed to compute monthly downtime minutes against the contractual SLA availability threshold. Finally, the deadline monitor subtracts the notice period from the renewal date to establish the urgency status.
  4. Stage 4: Cost Comparison & Recommendation Synthesis
    The cost comparator reads vendor quotations for Renew, Renegotiate, and Replace. It calculates net annual values, potential licence de-scoping savings, and SLA credit deductions. It validates whether the Replace quotation includes migration costs. The decision engine evaluates all metrics against deterministic rules to formulate the final recommendation.
  5. Stage 5: Audit Persistence & Visual Presentation
    The complete assessment result is serialized to an immutable JSON file, stamped with a SHA-256 fingerprint, and displayed dynamically across the seven tabs of the Streamlit dashboard.

6.2 Handling Ambiguity and Low-Confidence Extractions

A critical weakness of generative AI approaches is their propensity to fabricate plausible-sounding answers when data is missing. The IT Vendor Renewal Decision Support Agent is deliberately designed to acknowledge uncertainty:

  • If a term (such as SLA Credit Percentage) cannot be found via regex, it is assigned confidence: "NOT_FOUND" with value: "NOT FOUND".
  • If multiple conflicting dates appear without clear contextual qualifiers, the extractor flags the field with confidence: "LOW" or "MEDIUM".
  • In the user interface, any field with low or missing confidence is highlighted with a prominent yellow warning callout: "⚠️ Low or missing confidence for: SLA Credit %. Manual review recommended."
  • The financial engine never assumes zero or default values for unextracted financial terms without explicitly warning the user that manual input is required.

6.3 Verification of Pure Local Execution (Zero-LLM Design)

To confirm compliance with enterprise data privacy mandates, code inspection verifies that:

  1. No external HTTP/HTTPS calls are made during analytical execution (no calls to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, or other cloud APIs).
  2. No API keys or cloud service tokens exist in the runtime codebase.
  3. Execution latency is under 50 milliseconds for the complete analytical suite, proving that all processing is entirely local and deterministic.

Chapter 9: Security, Privacy, and Ethical Considerations

9.1 Commercial Confidentiality and Data Sovereignty

Enterprise software vendor contracts contain highly sensitive business data, including custom pricing discounts, volume tiers, confidential liability limitations, and intellectual property provisions. Inadvertent disclosure of these terms to competitors or third-party cloud providers can severely damage negotiating leverage or violate non-disclosure agreements (NDAs).

The IT Vendor Renewal Decision Support Agent addresses this risk through its purely local architecture. All document parsing, data analysis, and report generation take place within the host environment. No document text, metadata, or telemetry logs are ever transmitted over external networks. Organizations maintain complete data sovereignty and compliance with corporate governance standards.

9.2 Multi-Vendor Search Isolation Guardrails

When an organization assesses multiple competing vendors concurrently, cross-vendor information leakage is an acute risk. For example, if an analyst searches for "service credits" while reviewing Vendor B, the search engine must never return clauses extracted from Vendor A's agreement.

The system enforces strict multi-vendor isolation at the architectural level: 

# In retriever.py: Isolated search space enforcement
def search(self, query: str, vendor_name: str, top_k: int = 5) -> List[ClauseChunk]:
    vendor_chunks = [c for c in self._indexed_chunks if c.vendor.lower() == vendor_name.lower()]
    # BM25 ranking is strictly executed over vendor_chunks ONLY
    return rank_bm25(query, vendor_chunks, top_k=top_k)

This design ensures that clause retrieval spaces remain strictly partitioned, preventing accidental cross-contamination.

9.3 Ethical Governance: Advisory Boundary vs Autonomous Action

In recent years, the deployment of "autonomous AI agents" in enterprise workflows has introduced severe legal and operational hazards. An autonomous system that inadvertently emails a vendor with a termination notice, or automatically signs a binding auto-renewal, exposes the organization to catastrophic contractual liabilities.

Mandatory Governance Boundary: The IT Vendor Renewal Decision Support Agent is strictly governed by an Advisory-Only Policy. The system is intentionally architected without APIs, network hooks, or credentials capable of:
  • Signing digital agreements or approving renewal quotes.
  • Transmitting legal notices or termination letters to vendors.
  • Modifying active contracts or issuing binding commitments.
All strategic decisions, contractual notices, and purchase approvals remain the sole responsibility of authorized human executives.

Chapter 10: Limitations and Future Enhancements

10.1 Current System Limitations

While the project achieves its core architectural objectives, several operational boundaries exist:

  • Optical Character Recognition (OCR) Dependency: The current ingestion pipeline processes digital PDF text layers. Scanned, photocopied, or image-only contracts generate explicit warning flags indicating that digital conversion is required.
  • Regex-Driven Legal Heuristics: Term extraction is driven by curated regular expressions. While highly reliable for standard commercial contracts, highly bespoke, non-standard legal drafting may result in low-confidence scores requiring manual review.
  • Static Telemetry Schema: The data loader requires structured columns (e.g., user_id, last_login). Highly customized identity schemas require pre-formatting before ingestion.
  • Single-Host Deployment: The system is currently designed as a standalone desktop application or local team utility, lacking multi-tenant role-based access control (RBAC).

10.2 Proposed Future Enhancements (Future Work)

The following enhancements represent promising avenues for future research and development:

  1. Local Open-Weight LLM Integration (Offline Hybrid): Integrate small, local open-weight language models (e.g., LLaMA-3-8B or Mistral-7B running via Ollama/llama.cpp) to supplement regex extraction for highly complex legal prose—retaining 100% offline privacy while enhancing natural language comprehension.
  2. Embedded Tesseract OCR Pipeline: Incorporate an embedded OCR engine to automatically process scanned physical agreements without external pre-processing.
  3. Enterprise ITAM/SAM Connectors: Develop read-only API connectors for major IT Asset Management (ITAM) and Software Asset Management (SAM) platforms (e.g., ServiceNow, Flexera, Okta) for real-time telemetry streaming.
  4. Enterprise Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Introduce SAML/SSO authentication with role-tiered permissions (Viewer, Procurement Analyst, Legal Counsel, Executive Approver).
